# founder-vision

Surviving your first year is hard; surviving your tenth year is a different game
entirely.

Most new businesses that survive launch eventually stall or die because their
initial advantage gets copied, their key marketing channels get expensive, or a
deep-pocketed competitor moves in. Differentiation gets you through the front
door; structural Power is what lets you own the building.

This skill equips founders with four master frameworks of long-term corporate
strategy:
1. **Hamilton Helmer's 7 Powers**: Establishing durable barriers so competitors
   cannot arbitrage your profits away.
2. **McKinsey's Three Horizons**: Staging growth across months 0-18 (Beachhead),
   years 2-4 (Expansion & Vertical Integration), and years 5-10+ (Platform Standard).
3. **Jim Collins' Compounding Flywheel**: Building the self-reinforcing operational
   loop that accelerates momentum with every rotation.
4. **Nassim Taleb's Antifragility**: Inoculating the business against 10-year macro
   shocks, AI commoditization, platform shifts, and black swan crises.

## The doctrine

Framework summaries live in `${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/powers.md`:
- Hamilton Helmer's 7 Powers (The dual test of Benefit + Barrier, Power Progression).
- McKinsey's Three Horizons of Growth (H1 Core, H2 Adjacencies, H3 Platform).
- Jim Collins' Compounding Flywheel (Direct causality, self-reinforcing loop).
- Nassim Taleb's Antifragile Strategy (The Barbell, Via Negativa, 10-Year Premortem).

## Step 1: gather the foundations

Read existing documents in `founder/`:
- `founder/idea.md` (the core offering and value proposition).
- `founder/differentiation.md` (the Unique Mechanism, brown cow compromise, and concept-judo).
- `founder/competitors.md` (who the incumbents and substitutes are).
- `founder/cfo.md` and `founder/numbers.json` (Year 1 economics, margins, cash required).
- `founder/ops.md` (suppliers, staffing, tools, and operational constraints).

If any of these are missing, identify the business archetype and proceed with
grounded assumptions, noting what needs validation.

## Step 2: define the 10-Year North Star

State what the business looks like in Year 10 if every strategic bet succeeds:
1. **Category Definition**: What new category does the business lead or own?
2. **Scale & Market Role**: Is it an indispensable regional powerhouse, a vertical
   monopoly, a national brand, or a foundational infrastructure layer?
3. **Customer Transformation**: How has the customer's default habit or workflow
   permanently shifted around this business?

Write this in 2 to 3 concise, concrete paragraphs. Avoid empty buzzwords.

## Step 3: map the 7 Powers progression

Using Hamilton Helmer's framework from `powers.md`, determine how the business
transitions from early survival to structural defensibility:

1. **Current Power (Year 0 to 1)**:
   - Identify which early power shields the business right now (typically
     **Counter-Positioning** against bloated incumbents, or a **Cornered Resource**
     such as exclusive location, proprietary IP, or unique talent).
   - Explain both the **Benefit** (why it wins customers) and the **Barrier** (why
     incumbents are paralyzed or unable to match it).
2. **Emerging Power (Years 2 to 4)**:
   - Identify the power built during scaling (e.g., **Switching Costs**, **Scale
     Economies**, or local **Network Effects**).
   - What operational investments in H1 make this power inevitable in H2?
3. **Mature Power (Years 5 to 10+)**:
   - Identify the terminal moats (e.g., institutional **Process Power**, global
     **Network Effects**, or iconic **Branding**).
   - Explain why a well-funded rival with 10x capital could not replicate this
     moat in year 10.

## Step 4: construct the Three Horizons roadmap

Map the strategic evolution across McKinsey's Three Horizons:

| Horizon | Timeframe | Strategic Focus | Revenue & Operating Model | Critical Milestone / Exit Trigger |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **H1: Beachhead** | Months 0--18 | Defend & optimize core unit economics | Cash-generating single product / single location / proven wedge | Positive cash flow & proven payback velocity |
| **H2: Adjacencies** | Years 2--4 | Vertical integration & market expansion | Multi-unit, upstream supply control, recurring subscriptions | 40%+ revenue outside initial beachhead |
| **H3: Platform** | Years 5--10+ | Ecosystem standard & infrastructure | Licensing, B2B wholesale, data / API network, white-label | Industry standard utility or category hegemon |

Detail the specific actions, operational shifts, and capital requirements for
each horizon based on the numbers in `founder/cfo.md`.

## Step 5: design the Compounding Flywheel

Build the 4-to-6 step operational flywheel (Collins):
1. **Step 1 (The Catalyst)**: The foundational operational discipline that starts
   the motion (e.g., obsessive ingredient curation, rapid customer turnaround).
2. **Step 2 (The Customer Payoff)**: The inevitable direct result that delights the
   early buyer.
3. **Step 3 (The Business Asset)**: How customer delight translates into structural
   advantage (e.g., word-of-mouth referral, high density, proprietary data).
4. **Step 4 (The Cost / Quality Advantage)**: How that asset lowers unit costs,
   unlocks exclusive supplier pricing, or increases margins.
5. **Step 5 (Reinvestment & Acceleration)**: How the savings or profits are poured
   directly back into Step 1, spinning the wheel faster.

Verify:
- Does Step A *inevitably* cause Step B? (No magical leaps).
- Does the final step directly feed Step 1?
- Does every turn widen the moat against copycats?

## Step 6: the 10-Year Premortem and Antifragility

Conduct Taleb's 10-Year Premortem to inoculate the company against existential
threats:
1. **Threat 1: Technological Obsolescence / AI Commoditization**
   - Scenario: How could AI, software automation, or synthetic alternatives make
     the core product obsolete?
   - The Built-in Hedge: How the company uses physical touchpoints, proprietary
     data, or human brand trust to become immune or gain from this shift.
2. **Threat 2: Platform / Gatekeeper Seizure**
   - Scenario: How could a dominant landlord, ad network (Meta/Google), app store,
     or single key distributor destroy margins?
   - The Built-in Hedge: Direct-to-consumer ownership, multi-homed distribution,
     or owning critical physical real estate / intellectual property.
3. **Threat 3: Macro Contraction / Capital Drought**
   - Scenario: Prolonged economic recession or extreme inflation in key inputs.
   - The Built-in Hedge: Low fixed-overhead structure, flexible variable costs,
     and negative working capital cycles that thrive when debt-fueled rivals go bust.

## Rules

- No empty corporate cliches ("synergies", "world-class innovation", "leveraging AI").
- Every power must state BOTH the Benefit AND the Barrier. If there is no barrier,
  it is not a power; it is just a temporary feature.
- Ground the H1 and H2 plans in the real numbers from `founder/cfo.md` and operations
  from `founder/ops.md`.
- No em-dashes anywhere in the text (use double hyphens -- or colons).

Write `founder/vision.md` containing:
